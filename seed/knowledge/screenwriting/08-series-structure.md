# Episode and season structure for television series

**Read this when:** breaking an episode into acts, choosing an act count, placing act outs and cliffhangers, designing a teaser, weaving A/B/C stories, beating out a one-hour pilot, auditing a shapeless episode, reverse-engineering a show, or ordering a season.
**Source:** distilled 2026-09-12 from jtydhr88/screenwriting-skills `sw-series-structure` (method only); underlying books: Calvisi, Douglas, Oberg, Landau (1e 2014 / 2e 2021), Goldberg & Rabkin (2003), Miller, Rabkin (2017), Blum; act lengths measured from published West Wing, Succession, Sopranos, Downton Abbey and Fleabag scripts.

## 1. How series structure differs from film

1. Tighter form, so harder: film scenes 5-7 pages, TV target 2; a 4-page meeting is a red flag (Douglas).
2. The script simulates the cut episode (Calvisi).
3. The writer labels acts by hand; an unbroken pilot reads as cheating (Calvisi).
4. Slug lines are not dramatic scenes (Douglas). Oberg: new protagonist or goal = new dramatic scene; new place or time = new logistical scene. Count the dramatic ones.
5. Air length != script length: 45-48 broadcast minutes, yet a pilot needs 50-60 pages (Calvisi).
6. Acts are ad slots (Oberg: story format, logistical). Key judgment: act count is delivery format; the three-act (before / during / after one main dramatic action or evolution) is the design tool.
7. Production counts days in/out first; write only DAY or NIGHT (Miller).
8. Structure is inherited from the pilot (Calvisi):

| Breaking Bad | T | A1 | A2 | A3 | A4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot | 1-3 | 4-17 | 18-31 | 32-43 | 44-57 |
| #308 | 1-4 | 5-15 | 16-31 | 32-42 | 43-53 |

## 2. The episode container

**2.1 Broadcast four-act chain (Goldberg & Rabkin).** Act One: characters, conflict, stakes -> Act Two ends turning the story in a startling new direction -> Act Three ends with the hero's control revealed as false -> Act Four solves it. "Always four" is dated (five since 2006, ABC six, streaming unlabeled); the chain holds - break in four first, then cut (Miller, Landau).

**2.2 Page anchors (Douglas, four acts + teaser).** Ads every 13-15 minutes. US one-hour only; never scale (checklist 4-5 likewise).
| Act 1 | Act 2 | Act 3 | Act 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| p. 17-18 | p. 30 | p. 45 | ~p. 60 (55-65) |

**2.3 Scene counts.**
| Source | Rule |
|---|---|
| Douglas | 14-min act / 2-min scenes = 7 per act; 5 can fill one; episodic 9-10. Four-act hour ~28; five/six-act ~20 |
| Oberg | A ~50% (14-15), B ~1/3 (9-10), C rest (4-5), ~30 total; modern scene ~1 min, key scenes 3-4 |
| Chase | ~35 beats: ~13 each in A and B, 5-6 in C, a few in D |

**2.4 Four -> five / six.** Douglas: stretch the teaser to ~10 pages as Act One; ~10 pages per act in five, ~8 in six; six-act tail = close arcs in Act Five, a short Act Six opens the next question. Blake: split the longest act. Miller: split Acts Three and Four; each new break needs a hard event. Five-act test: find the two shortest acts.

**2.5 Teaser / cold open.** Length: Douglas 1-10 min; Landau ~5 pages; Oberg 3-5 min, ending on cliffhanger or surprise. Ten types (Douglas 7 + Landau 3): world portrait without the lead | anomaly the lead does not know | arriving after the fact | the case first | ensemble's day begins | thematic tie | environment cue | bookend | (seemingly) innocuous | DPU (direct pickup). Open with story, not information (Douglas). Teaser flashback never answers the dramatic question; borrow from before or during the climax (Oberg). Not mandatory: many start in Act One or use a short Act One as teaser (Landau 2e, Oberg). Cold open != teaser (Miller): a thematic one leaves the point of attack in Act One.

**2.6 Invisible acts: three tests.** Unlabeled premium scripts still ride the 4-6-act template (Calvisi). Measured on Succession: (1) story-day / time-block markers - new day = new act; (2) change of location or vehicle; (3) the moment the core question turns. Criterion: each act ends on a transfer of information or power; a hook is optional. S4: 8/10 episodes sit in a container whose fixed programme replaces act breaks; closed day 34-42 scenes, multi-location 76-78.

**2.7 ACT on the page (keep both).** Landau 2e: break by acts, strip labels - yet showrunners often want them as proof. Miller: broadcast-able content must label. Mazzara: labels force compression. Rule: broadcast / basic cable / AVOD -> label; premium / SVOD -> break in four, strip or use day markers.

**2.8 Page counts and lengths** (date every number).
| Source | Number |
|---|---|
| Calvisi | one-hour pilot 54-58 pp, cap 60 |
| Landau | one-hour 48-63; fast shows 85+ |
| Miller | drama pilot 55-65 |
| Blum | drama ~60, teaser + four |

## 3. Act outs, cliffhangers, information tools

**3.1 Test.** Calvisi: every act (ideally scene) pulls the reader on; the last beat is the act out. Landau 2e: cliffhanger = danger or shocking revelation; Oberg: any unresolved conflict, also before leaving a strand. Anti-pattern: schmuck bait. Landau 2e: forcing a cliffhanger on every break is deadly; land on a turning point grown from character jeopardy, risk or fear; a surprise must feel should-have-seen-it. Hours hold the big one for the end; multi-cam puts it mid-episode. Resolve within the season.

**3.2 Ten cliffhangers (Landau).** 2e: character in peril | ticking bomb | love in the balance | surprise twist / aha | death (resets the board) | discovery of a secret | life events. 1e: lack of closure (too ambiguous = backlash) | mini-cliffhangers = act outs | comedy cliffhangers (balance broken, Act Two restores). Moves: reverse with one word; hold the reveal, never exit on the joke.

**3.3 Eight act-out forms; three laws.** West Wing: suspense question / reversal line / emotional reveal / wordless action / prop freeze / repeated line inverted / cut-off setup / planted gesture paid off. Each out to a different strand; at least one wordless or prop out, grammar taught earlier. An out can be pure information (Goldberg & Rabkin). Three laws (3, as in the source): (1) an act ends with the hero in a no-retreat position, not the biggest crisis; (2) the Act Two break changes the story's nature, not just danger; (3) Act Three's end turns a win into a loss.

**3.4 Suspense and stakes.** Douglas: anticipation -> expectation -> surprise, then a new unit. Landau 2e: suspense = anticipation + surprise. Stakes test: if doing nothing is viable, stakes are insufficient. Mark +/- per act; acts may end on a win.

**3.5 Four information tools (Oberg).** Character = point; + want = line; + need = plane; + managing information (who knows what when) = solid.
| Tool | Definition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Dramatic irony | audience knows, a character (victim) does not | -> suspense; antagonist as victim; ironic question: how/when will the victim learn? |
| Surprise | sudden unexpected information | raises stakes; most gags |
| Mystery | enough to know you do not know | slow burn; sours if long - one per season, solved and replaced |
| Suspense | audience aware of danger | needs no irony if the threat's source is known |
Four red lines (4, as in the source): (1) never let the hero out-know the audience for long, esp. motive backstory - catch up fast or make the hero ignorant too; (2) cliffhangers by information bomb, not a cut-off fight; (3) closed mystery (all ignorant) vs open mystery = dramatic irony; (4) mystery about the antagonist's nature, never about the reality of the danger.

**3.6 Pacing (Rabkin).** A twist that undoes the last scene is fake movement; change plot, character and story every 7-8 minutes. Spec pilot: move the Act Four event to the teaser's end and hold the pace. Every episode needs a next-day-talk scene. Streaming drops previously-on, recaps, post-ad resets.

## 4. A/B/C/runner weaving

**4.1 Definitions.** Douglas: three independent stories, same arena; A most resonant (not longest), C comic or runner. Calvisi: letters by screen time, never in the script; strand = character + goal + obstacle. Landau: A = franchise, B = personal, runners = C/D. Oberg: A+B minimum, 4-5 max; only A and B get three acts; runners 3-5 beats.

**4.2 Rules.**
1. A runs through every act; 4-5 beats per act, closing on A (Landau).
2. Never close an act on C - if it earns a break it is A or B (Landau, Oberg).
3. The hook may still be a B turn (Douglas).
4. No checkerboard; follow the line of interest (Douglas).
5. Cutting away makes ellipsis.
6. No exposition-only scene; facts in an emotional context (Douglas).
7. Plan strands apart; weave in the teleplay (Douglas).
8. Cross on a thematic link, family strongest; runners can grow into main lines; serials need not touch everyone; elliptical storytelling tells a runner in the cut (Landau 1e).

**4.3 Procedural (Donahue).** A investigation / B emotional, alternate constantly; a warm body within 17 pages; each suspect yields something that builds.

**4.4 Alternatives (keep alongside).** West: break by character, then weave; reversals at p. 40-45 of 55. Mazzara: reject A/B/C, ask what the episode is about; cost - minor characters stay minor. Oberg: strands sharing one goal are one co-protagonist A. Fellowes: 8-12 strands, one scene advances six; keep one unrelated to the crisis.

**4.5 Grid (Douglas); Oberg's steps.** Columns T | 1-4, rows 1-7 slots, one letter per cell. Name strands; hang each on a lead with a one-sentence logline (story, not situation); fill. Reverse-engineer: opening, 3/4 worst point, ending; A or A+B. Oberg: Mapping -> Sequencing (per strand: action, inciting incident, climax, three acts, Act 2 cut by subgoal) -> Weaving (marks X, BA2/BA3, CH, MAC); an hour holds ~5 sequences.

## 5. Pilot beat sheet (Calvisi Story Map)

Benchmark: Teaser 2-10 pp -> Act One 12-15 -> Act Two 6-10 (~p. 30) -> Act Three 8-12 (p. 40) -> Act Four 6-12 (p. 48) -> Act Five 2-8.
Story Map (18 beats, as in the source chain): 1 Opening Sequence; 2 Catalyst (teaser out); 3 A/B/C/D set up + New Arrival / Shadow / Dynamic Ally; 4 Inciting Incident; 5 Turn / Roadblock (Act One out); 6 Aftermath; 7 First Trial / First Casualty; 8 New Complications; 9 Midpoint (Act Two out, ticking clock); 10 Aftermath; 11 Shadow Showdown; 12 Partial Integration; 13 Assumption of Power (Act Three out, p. 37-40); 14 Skirmishes / Integration / Stopping Points; 15 Declaration of War + All Is Lost (Act Four out, ~4/5); 16 Climax (A, B); 17 Epilogue / New World; 18 Hanging Question / Cliffhanger.
Checks: AOP p. 37-40; DOW ~4/5; TV-only beats = Assumption of Power, Declaration of War, Shadow Showdown, First Trial. Form, not formula. Act One works as a closed story with a cliffhanger. Too many strands = dense, too few hides the 100-episode story; end well, pay off nothing fully.
Miller's six landmarks: point of attack (teaser) -> hero committed (Act One out) -> succeeds, almost (midpoint; not "fails") -> emotional squeeze (two bad options; poor act out) -> succeeds with twist -> new life. Tragedy: squeeze positive, climax negative.
Landau's three pilot endings: launch the franchise; final montage; final discovery - withhold. Pilot types -> knowledge/screenwriting/09-series-engine-and-bible.md.

## 6. Five-part scene (Miller, after Gentile)

Want / obstacle / escalations / decision / resolution. Want and obstacle precede the scene; the first escalation opens it; decision = climax; resolution carries into the next scene. 3+ escalations, prefer action (all-oral = radio; measured 14-15 in 1:57, 11 in 1:40). One hero per scene, may change; a solo scene's opposition is the world. No want = a moment, not a scene. Series specs: 2-page target; emotion over appearance; ANGLE marks a new beat; do not micromanage actors; stage directions may carry subtext.

## 7. Season shape

| Measured (pages) | T | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| West Wing "17 People" (bottle) | 6 | 14 | 10 | 10 | 18 | 58 |
| West Wing "Two Gunmen" I | 7 | 21 | 14 | 11 | 14 | 67 |
| West Wing "Two Gunmen" II | 2 | 11 | 14 | 16 | 18 | 61 |
| West Wing "Two Cathedrals" | 3 | 13 | 13 | 12 | 17 | - |
| Sopranos "Pine Barrens" | 64 pp, 71 scenes |
| Downton S2 acts per episode | 6, 4, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 6; special 7 |
| Fleabag episode | cold open 1-3 min -> titles -> 3-7 segments -> button <= 1 page |
| Stranger Things S1 | logistical 3-3-2 episodes; dramatic Act 1 ~10 min, Act 2 371, Act 3 13 |

7.1 Logistical and dramatic layers coexist (Oberg); one dramatic sequence per strand per episode. Starting too early? Cut an episode; start in medias res (Landau 2e).
7.2 Tentpoles (Landau 1e): teaser + act breaks + tag (epilogue button, runner payoff or B cliffhanger).
7.3 Bottle = budget (no guests, locations, sets, extras). Outs may deepen instead of turn; strand count is the rhythm (4 normal, 5 bottle, 7 finale); invert bans into one night, one building, a truth-telling beat on a hidden season secret. Bottle != one strand.
7.4 Container and ritual episodes (Succession): a communication limit makes information move by body; a ritual gives a free beat sheet, one conflict per stage; an absent protagonist is the strongest suspense hold.
7.5 Two-parter: part one alternates timelines; part two closes every out in the present; one-scene teaser; one prop stitches both.
7.6 Finales. Downton: last act pure pay-off plus streamers; open accounts clear before the penultimate act. Succession S1: finale mirrors the pilot's turn with the same number; each landing creates next season's debt. Sopranos: anti-climactic. Sorkin: images first, one object across three acts; a finale teaser only sets the deadline.
7.7 Six-episode shape (Fleabag): 1 lay out everyone and the question -> 2 feeling into desire -> 3 first card shown -> 4 bottle, two people, a prohibition -> 5 false-victory peak -> 6 one closed venue, all strands close; button resolves theme. One object changes hands each episode.
7.8 Multi-strand (Downton S2): multi-season spine, season arcs, 1-3-episode guests; regulars never leave, only outsiders die; make the audience guess someone else first.
7.9 Alternatives (Landau 2e): linear, flashback (bookend), zigzag, hopscotch, three timelines, Rashomon, time loop, backwards. Selection questions (5, as in the source): (1) how much time does the story cover? (2) must the audience hold far-future information? (3) does reordering raise tension? (4) does it fit the story's underlying experience? (5) do you truly want it? Default linear. Time planes as a tonal valve (Pina); Mazzara opposes flashbacks in horror.

## 9. Diagnostic checklist
(19 questions, as in the source)
1. Core question in one sentence?
2. Every strand a logline on a lead, not a guest - story, not situation?
3. A in every act; breaks on A or B; any act closing on C?
4. 4-5 beats and 5-7 scenes per act; 20 (five/six acts) to 28-30 (four) total? (US one-hour only.)
5. (US one-hour only) Anchors 17-18 / 30 / 45 / 60; Calvisi teaser <= 10, 30, 40, 48; AOP 37-40; worst point 4/5?
6. Any scene over 3 pages that is not one beat?
7. Any exposition-only scene; any want-less moment used as a scene?
8. Five parts in every scene (3+ escalations, mostly action); hero's emotional position clear at the end?
9. Each act out nameable as an event; grown from jeopardy/risk/fear; surprise set up?
10. How many of the eight forms; one wordless or prop out, grammar taught?
11. Act Two break changes the story's nature; Act Three's end turns a win into a loss?
12. Is doing nothing viable; +/- per act; any act ending on a win?
13. Where are the four information tools; does the hero out-know the audience; season mystery resolved?
14. Teaser story or message; does a teaser flashback answer the dramatic question?
15. Last act pay-off only (finales); open accounts clear before the penultimate act; something to discover without paying all off?
16. Streaming: labels stripped, do the three tests locate the acts; a transfer of information or power per act end?
17. Right slot in the six-episode shape; finale mirrors pilot; each landing creates next season's debt?
18. Can the Act Four event move to the teaser; a next-day-talk scene?
19. Int/ext and day/night ratios; only DAY/NIGHT; limits inverted into bottle choices?

## 10. Workflow (14 steps, as in the source)
1. Fix series type, story-type, format (Oberg) -> knowledge/screenwriting/09-series-engine-and-bible.md.
2. Pick a structural comp. Four questions (4, as in the source): teaser? how many breaks? scenes per act? tag? Landau 1e's 47-show sample (2014): unlabeled = HBO/Showtime/Netflix/PBS only (12); teaser + 4 (13); teaser + 5 (4); 5 acts (1); 6 acts, all broadcast/basic cable (5); 7 (1); multi-cam cold open + 2 + tag (3); single-cam (2); unrecorded (6, one of them long teaser + 4 in the text). Procedurals = non-premise pilots; premise pilots cluster in serials.
3. Grid two or three comp episodes.
4. Set the core question and each strand's entry and exit scene.
5. Reverse-engineer: opening, 3/4 worst point, ending; A or A+B.
6. Per strand: landmarks (Miller) or inciting incident -> subgoal -> sequence -> scene (Oberg). Keep apart.
7. One-page grid (Miller): one card per scene, heading + want; ad-free shows break teaser + four, strip later.
8. Assign act outs: different strand each, one wordless/prop; squeeze stays mid-act.
9. A procedural metronome: an external deadline that licenses interruptions; never carries the out.
10. Outline / beat sheet: Blum treatment 5-10 pages with act breaks (film 10-20); each beat = a scene logline -> knowledge/screenwriting/10-writers-room-and-notes.md.
11. Weave in the teleplay: layering, ellipsis, cross-cutting; add the five parts.
12. Overwrite then cut (70+ pages) vs hold under 60 - showrunner's call.
13. Format: label or strip (2.7); SMASH CUT TO: MAIN TITLES; FADE OUT + END OF ACT N; pilots carry no scene numbers (Miller).
14. Run section 9, then look for the lies (Paley via Landau).

## 11. Training (14 exercises, as in the source)
1. Grid one episode; mark which strand owns each hook, starts late, ends early.
2. G&R pair: one sentence of arc per act plus its last scene; compare Act Two breaks.
3. Classify five act outs by the eight forms; spot the schmuck bait.
4. Story Map a pilot; then your own.
5. Miller landmark sheet; break a structurally similar pilot.
6. Count escalations in two short scenes (aim 10+); action vs speech.
7. Locate invisible acts in an unlabeled episode with the three tests.
8. Rewrite one cut-off out as surprise, dramatic irony, mystery.
9. Convert a four-act outline to five, six and label-free, a real event per new break.
10. Move the Act Four event to the teaser; rewrite at that pace.
11. Write a bottle under four bans; strands 4 -> 5.
12. Six-episode season on the Fleabag skeleton with a hand-changing object.
13. Symmetry: a pilot number/object/line the finale turns.
14. One pressure-chamber episode and one ritual-day episode.
