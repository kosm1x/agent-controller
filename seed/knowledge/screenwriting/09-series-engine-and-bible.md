## 1. Defining the engine
1. Franchise = concept + conflict + theme + story pattern (Rabkin); a trait list cannot build one.
2. Theme is never one word (Rabkin): an arguable opposition.
3. Theme must land as conflict between people; concept is a replaceable container.
4. Mesh test (Rabkin): one story exploring concept AND conflict, then 99 more.
5. Jazz, not the novel: conflict + theme are the chord changes; episodes improvise.
6. Phrasings. Landau: tacit contract, same yet different weekly; franchise = what the lead DOES. Goldberg & Rabkin: a rule set licensing invention. Douglas: each episode needs a springboard (case or personal inciting incident). Oberg: an engine of questions. Miller / Calvisi: license to intervene; Compelling Crisis + Week-to-Week.
7. Story pattern = the shape every episode repeats (Rabkin): one sentence, run in the pilot's second half.
8. Five legs tests: 99 stories (Rabkin) / six episodes (Landau 1e) / three per pitch, five-six per presentation (Landau 2e, Blum) / 100 episodes + four questions (Goldberg & Rabkin) / five stories (Blum). Closed forms use 10.
9. Concept strength does not predict sustainability (Torchwood): no third episode = a character-conflict fault.
10. Engine-pressure test (Succession S4 foreword): end where the material plot engine loses pressure, not where characters run dry; argue the count from it. Substitute for 8.

## 2. Writing the engine as sentences
11. Central question = process sentence (Rabkin): how X keeps doing Y in a world that Z.
12. Do not play out the central mystery in the pilot (Rabkin).
13. Story landmines (Rabkin): character conflicts in pilot subtext that detonate later; one per major character (unspoken fear, desire or contradiction), episode set. Many mines, ONE master suspense.
14. Suffering is not conflict (Feed the Beast): two attributes of one person must obstruct each other.
15. Sweet spot (Landau) = genre, tone, theme, conflict, franchise, central question crossing; 2e: character x franchise.
16. Marketing-intern test (Rabkin, optional): could a junior publicist sell your sentence? Big = conflict scale.
17. Big need not mean shallow: enlarge by profession, place or era. Miller: characters beat high concept.
18. Ledger units (this pack): each solved thing leaves a debt a later episode pays (6-10 per season); pattern = shape, ledger = consequence.
19. Segment by the Nth use of the same skill, not by calendar (this pack, on Landau's movements): win -> investigate -> invert -> change the rules.

## 3. Character web
20. Externalise inner conflict as a ring of pulling people (Rabkin).
21. Every series is a family show (Landau), imperfect and discontented.
22. Familial-role library (Landau): authority, siblings, spouses, elders, prodigals, black sheep, child-adults; fix each pair's +/- vector.
23. Transactional family (Landau): the boss's withheld approval replaces a parent's.
24. Scene rules (Landau): who loves you most can hurt you most (which button?); a newcomer must change the regulars.
25. Specialisation (Landau) makes conflict, POVs, legibility; Out-of-Their-Element generator.
26. Rational axis (Rabkin): attraction needs a structural reason, not chemistry.
27. Complementary duo (Landau): two incomplete people make one whole; need denied out of shame.
28. Sakamoto's drift: the value turn comes by kindness, not confrontation.
29. Shame and voids (Landau): trauma -> voids -> coping; its challenger is an antagonist.
30. Grounded superpower that is also the liability (Landau): keep the lead there.
31. Same backstory told twice (Landau): edited first, true later; the gap is the shame.
32. Flaw cadence (Landau): flaws root in backstory; the pilot only hints.
33. Name the antagonistic force before giving it a face (Landau); Suber: villains are antagonists, not the reverse.
34. Every villain is the hero of his own story; the goal attained ends the series unless it pivots.
35. No straw men (the Kings): drama = liked regulars disagreeing, each with a point.
36. Sustainable-web kit (Blum): bond pattern + rubbing traits + a situation that stops leaving.

## 4. Pilot types and strategy
37. Types (Landau; others rename them):

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| premise | ep 1's event is needed to start from day one |
| non-premise / ongoing / midstream | our first day, not the characters' (Succession) |
| hybrid | open on the climax then flash back, or an outside event hits an old relationship (Sopranos) |
| semi-cold start (Oberg) | teaser -> flashback -> lead wakes as ignorant as we are |

38. Entry rules: Oberg - never hold the inciting incident to the end; Calvisi - late in the world, early in the script.
39. Strategies (Blum, dated): a premise pilot is a poor barometer; half-and-half / refuse premise / pure premise. Smith: executives prefer typical-episode. Landau: no trap, declare which. Rules: sitcom -> typical; rules-heavy world -> premise; web-engine -> midstream; both -> Breaking Bad shape.
40. Three endings: the pilot's end begins the series (Landau); stick the landing on portended conflict; cliffhanger optional (Miller), never pay all off (Calvisi).
41. Douglas's four steps: create the world -> find the springboards -> populate it -> make a plan (first ten pages decide). Core = a 100-hour secret.
42. Ten anxious questions (Jeffries): list ten at pilot's end; each becomes a future episode.
43. Sopranos: the pilot withholds Tony's position, so needs no cliffhanger.

## 5. Series types and story-types (Oberg)
44. | Type | Note |
|---|---|
| procedural | plot-led episodes, any order, widest audience |
| serial | any season story-type; episodes keep a plot-led A line |
| serial-procedural hybrid (3) | mythology episodes / unit per season / serial-heavy |
| sitcom | characters do not change; fine |
| limited (4-8) | easiest step from film |
| anthology | each reset = a chance to quit |
| miniseries series | see 45 |
| web series | - |

45. Miniseries-series rules (Rabkin): concept not defined by execution; each season opener re-hooks. Landau 2e: limited sells harder; 10 episodes/season benchmark.
46. Story-type: where does the season's main problem come from? external -> plot-led; internal -> character-led (needs a catalyst); society -> theme-led; plus hybrid, exception.
47. Reverse check: one protagonist, one season-level action -> not theme-led; lines sharing one goal and trigger -> plot-led.
48. Wrong combination boxes you in: resolving a character-led inner problem in S1 forces a switch that rarely works.
49. Maslow and M-Factor: lower main problem = larger audience; explore vertically. M-Factor (1-5) = fit of audience, genre, story-type, budget.
50. What's at stake?: failure must cause sadness, anxiety or fear; no episode without nameable stakes.
51. Arena defects (Miller, 2015): school has a clock; family gets asked "what's episode 44?"; relationship shows admit few newcomers; reporters lack license (agency completes the engine).

## 6. Documents: logline to bible
52. Blum's tiers: concept (arena, characters, conflict) -> presentation (+ pilot premise, sample stories) -> bible (+ pilot script, arcs, outlines).
53. Page specs: bible 10-85 pp, mini-bible ~5, mythology <= 1 page (Landau); verbal pitch 20 min; pitch bible 10-30 pp, ~15 (Oberg); treatment drama 10-15, comedy 8-10, production bible 70-100 (Miller); sitcom format 10-15 pp (Smith); Douglas's bible tiers: ~100 pp / a few pages / none, accumulated.
54. Four field sets, pick one: Landau 2e Essential Guideposts (~25 fields; logline must contain "but"; question = future, mystery = past) / Landau's 11-item pitch recipe: why this idea, why you, why now, tone = genre, teaser first, whole pilot, characters embedded, mythology <= 1 page, three future episodes, stick the landing / Miller's treatment / Calvisi's 17-column Basic Story Map (Compelling Crisis, Week-to-Week, Achilles Heel, Shadow, Pilot Arc...), filled per episode and per series.

| Document | Fields |
|---|---|
| Logline (Oberg, Landau) | <= 3 lines; who wants what, why, obstacle, cost; contains "but" |
| Springboard (Douglas, Smith) | per-episode starter; formats list 8-10 concept-funny loglines; serials thread arcs |
| Pitch document, Rabkin's 10 | 1 overview, one page, a buy-line per genre; 2 hero: want + weekly task; 3 opposite number; 4 how we got here: synopsis ending on the series normal; 5 life in the world: had / has; 6 the other way: co-lead's view; 7 weekly reality; 8 cast: function + theme position + landmine; 9 antagonist: motive from theme; 10 sample story areas: 5-6 |
| Series format, Smith's 11 (sitcom) | 1 title page; 2 opening sell copy; 3 premise 1-3 pp: continuing conflict source; 4 pilot story 1-2 pp; 5 8-10 springboards; 6 cast, a paragraph each; 7 market analysis; 8 budget; 9 unique aspects; 10 credits; 11 closing copy. 2, 7-11 optional |
| Treatment, Miller's 10 | title; type; idea; logline <= 30 words; world; characters; story engine; themes; tone; three season arcs or 10 episode loglines; six questions: relevance, difference, envelope, research, influences, why me |
| Bible, Oberg's 12 | 1 title <= 3 words; 2 logline <= 3 lines; 3 overview: not a pilot summary; 4 breakdowns: 3-5 leads <= 1 page each; 5 story world; 6 pilot story 3-5 pp; 7 S1 arc with end hook; 8 series arc optional; 9 tone, genre, style; 10 episode loglines, serial 8-15; 11 package; 12 pitch deck = visual bible |

55. Five commonest errors: overview as pilot summary (write world, quest, springboards); character laundry list; telling everything (success = they ask); over-planning (90% into S1); tone mismatched to genre.
56. Storyline document skeleton (this pack):

| Field | Fill | Test |
|---|---|---|
| name | driver + verb | abstract noun = no want |
| purpose | its one job | cannot say -> cut |
| driver | who has most conflict | shares goal + trigger -> a strand of A |
| stakes | failure + Maslow level | cannot say -> cut |
| episode entry | scene + trigger | after midpoint -> not here |
| episode exit | state + who knows what | must shift knowledge |
| season beats | 3-8 | two silent episodes = forgotten |
| controlling idea | which side, when it switches | theme filter |
| causality with other lines | affects / affected by | pull test: no effect -> cut |
Landau's story tentacles: break toward more story.

## 7. Adapting IP (Landau 2e unless noted)
1 Epics suit streaming; comics are episodic. 2 Chain of title first; structure is not copyrightable; Smith: sell only what you own. 3 What a page tolerates a screen may not. 4 No formula; an era and a theme per season. 5 Change characters, era, place with a reason. 6 Honour the spirit: composites, cut subplots. 7 Start where the gap triggers flashback. 8 Core = who knows what when. 9 Externalise interiority: POVs or unreliable narrator. 10 World document or story?

## 9. Diagnostics
**Engine** (10 questions, as in the source): 1 theme an arguable opposition? 2 landed between two people? 3 mesh test: a 2nd and 6th story? 4 process sentence? 5 pattern in one sentence, run in the pilot? 6 follow-ons differ in source and ending? 7 a hundred episodes? (closed forms: pressure test) 8 stakes nameable, Maslow level? 9 lead has license? 10 one-liner says more than "it's good"?
**Character web** (10 questions, as in the source): 11 a mine per character, episode set? 12 two attributes interfering? 13 suffering taken for conflict? 14 force named before a face? 15 villain the hero of his story? 16 vector per pair? 17 newcomers change the regulars? 18 superpower also liability? 19 a backstory told twice? 20 next mission after the goal?
**Pilot and documents** (10 questions, as in the source; skip for one aired episode): 21 which pilot type, why? 22 mystery played out? 23 ten questions -> ten episodes? 24 franchise shown? 25 overview a pilot summary; springboards? 26 characters embedded or listed? 27 tone fits genre? 28 told everything? 29 S5 fixed? 30 S1 resolves the inner problem?
**Reader** (1 question, as in the source): 31 who is this page for? Tool sheets and submission documents: write both, submit one.
**Debt and segmentation** (2 questions, as in the source): 32 each solved thing leaves a later debt (18)? 33 segmented by skill use or by calendar (19)?

## 10. Workflow: idea to bible
1 Research the arena (Miller). 2 Three whys (Landau): idea, you, now. 3 Dig the world (Douglas). 4 Find the engine: franchise + springboard; verb = micro mission. 5 Rabkin's derivation: research -> flip (outsider lead) -> sharpen -> clash of equals -> one exit -> intrinsic conflict -> evergreen theme -> revise concept. 6 Fix the type (Oberg): format -> series type -> story-type -> genre -> M-Factor. 7 Build the web: roles -> vectors -> specialisations -> mines -> force. 8 Calvisi's 17 columns. 9 Treatment (Miller) or presentation (Blum). 10 5-6 follow-ons; same shape -> step 4. 11 One page per storyline (56), pull-test. 12 Pilot: type (39) -> first ten pages -> pattern run -> mines -> ten questions -> script. 13 Pitch document / mini-bible. 14 Rehearse 20 minutes, three future episodes (-> 10). 15 Living bible post-sale.

## 11. Exercises
1 A night of TV: <= 4 lines per show on what it is. 2 Three episodes of one show: what only it could tell. 3 20 loglines in 30 minutes; under 6 -> rebuild. 4 Five shows' questions as process sentences. 5 Mine table. 6 Pair vector table; a third blank = no web. 7 Blum's five stories, source and ending marked. 8 Calvisi twice. 9 Ten questions -> ten loglines. 10 A novel -> era and theme per season, starting point + three reasons. 11 Engine-pressure check: when does it fail?
