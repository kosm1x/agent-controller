# Half-hour comedy: predicament machines, joke mechanics and format
**Read this when:** sitcom episodes, specs and pilots; joke density; predicament design; multi- vs single-cam and page counts; cold open / acts / tag; punch-ups; dramedy.

## 1. Half-hour vs one-hour: the switch table (Smith, Landau 1e, Blum)
| Dimension | Half-hour | One-hour |
|---|---|---|
| Pages | film/single 27-30; tape/multi 45-50; animation 40-50 | 48-63 |
| Acts | cold open + 2 (multi) or 2-4 (single) + tag | teaser + 4-6 |
| Act length | teaser 30-60 s; acts 10-12 min; tag <=1 min | 10-15 min (Blum 2001) |
| Act One | 1-5 of 30 pp; world pre-built | ~1/4 |
| Scenes | multi 15-20/ep, 3-5/act, long; single 40-50 (up to 80) short | by format |
| Sets | 3 ideal (1 main + 2), max 5 | many |
| Unit | beats: A 5-9, subplot 3-5, thread 3-6 | scenes, act outs |
| Density | 2-4 real laughs/page; multi: joke almost every line | comic viewpoint |
| Stakes | small but vital ("tremendous trifle") | life/career |
| Silence | multi: dead air; single: cringe allowed | a resource |
| Growth | weekly lesson and reset; expansion, not change | season arcs |

Engine = relationship density, not events (Blum).

## 2. Premise-driven comedy: three levels (Smith)
**2.1 Level one, four sources:** predicaments, character mix, style of comedy, casting. A premise predicament yields laughs until resolved and makes unrelated jokes land harder; preconditions: nobody is really hurt, it is known as comedy.
- **Nine predicaments:** Big Lie, Big Secret, Misunderstanding, Rock and a Hard Place, Surefire Scheme, Something That Rocks the Boat, Time Bomb, Trapped in a Space, Sex; tenth: Dead Body. Reusable when fresh; each dies when resolved.
- **Six mixes:** Role Reversal, Fish Out of Water, Odd Coupling, Sex, Pretense (posing, then exposure), a sixth left open in 2009. Complement, never duplicate; some are funny only as foils.
**2.2 Every show is its own genre:** the premise sets expectations. Errors: Too Funny to Pass Up; Genre Shuffle. An episode expands, never changes, the premise.
**2.3 Level two:** compound predicaments (stacked or mixed; finite, real-time); stir the mix (temporary alliance, order restored); escalate to a comedy climax; check laugh strength per sequence before dialogue.

## 3. Joke mechanics (level three)
Setup implies a complication/incongruity (tension); punchline resolves it from an unexpected direction (release).
**3.1 Setup:** 1 plausible, ordinary; 2 two parts: topic + 1-2 beats of development (carries character truth); 3 parts may be scattered; 4 never hint the punch; 5 shared experience can be part one.
**3.2 Punchline:** 1 punch word last; 2 don't go past the joke; 3 topper: top at most twice, rare in dramedy; 4 running gag/callback returns 2-3 times, last reversed; 5 stuck: rewrite the setup; 6 go literal.
**3.4 Nuances** (ten; eight named): Understated Response, Exaggerated Response, Irony, Nervous Babbling, Pulls No Punches, Space-Shot POV, Wordplay, Double Entendre. A character's fixed way of being funny; assign 1-2 each.
**3.5 General:** hang a lantern on implausibility; harvest every seed; bigger is better; small truths, big laughs; Reiss: funny, serves story, character stays likeable.
**3.6 Density:** 2-4 real laughs/page, red-pen count, opening scenes first; not too jokey; even quantity and type; toppers and running gags are the cheapest lift.

## 4. Half-hour structure
**4.1 Beat sheet (one page):** A 5-9, subplot 3-5, thread 3-6; a beat changes goal or raises tension, starts with the lead's name (Sandler).
**4.2 Single-A three acts (film, 30 pp):** pages 1-5 / 15-20 (development, reveal + choice) / 5-10.
> Conflicts with the 10-12-min acts above: fix act count first. Two acts (multi): 10-12 min each. Three-act single-A: 1-5/15-20/5-10. Pilots run a longer Act One.
**4.4 Dramatic vs broadcast:** ad breaks need not match dramatic acts; move plot points before the usual breaks; strong act breaks score in a spec.
**4.5 Landau's seven steps:** 1 ordinary world broken by a problem (cold open; tremendous trifle); 2 Act One: game plan, B/C problems; 3 one theme unifies A/B/C; 4 midpoint reversal + raised stakes (end of Act One in two-act, Act Two in three-act): the fix makes a bigger problem; 5 all-is-lost; 6 resolved by honest communication and/or amends; 7 optional tag: safe and content.
**4.6 Key dramatic moment (Williams):** one per episode, nine in ten inside the block comedy scene; fix it first.
**4.8 Six-episode season (Fleabag):** cold open > TITLES > 3-8 segments > button. 1 cast and hooks laid out; 2 E1's feeling becomes a want; 3 first card shown at a family event; 4 bottle episode: closed space + one prohibition breached step by step, last breach emotional; 5 false-victory high; 6 all recalled to one closed event. Finale button resolves theme, not plot; one object changes hands every episode.

## 5. Characters
- Drive, purpose, soft spot, skill, not likeability; troublemakers are liked best.
- Proximity counts: a device must squeeze the cast together; conflict between regulars.
- Miller: situations aren't funny, people are; complementary traits: each described positively, any pair yields jokes, every viewpoint valid.
- Ten behavioural roles (Supportive Parent, Idiot Savant, Idiot Idiot, Clown, Operator, Mentor, Confidant, Irritant, Romantic Interest, Critic): temporary modes.
- Fleabag: cast named by one trait, hero nameless.

## 6. Three formats and page targets
Film 27-30 pp (page = minute), mixed-case directions, single-spaced dialogue. Tape 45-50 pp, ALL-CAPS directions, underlined entrances/exits, double-spaced dialogue, lettered scenes on new pages. Animation 40-50 pp, film-like, double-spaced dialogue, 2-3 acts by show; absurd situations, realistic reactions (Cohen). A spec omits scene numbers, cast lists, sound slugs, camera directions, draft labels, dates; copy the target show's scripts. Sets (Blum 2001; Smith 2009): 3 ideal, 5 max; 3-5 standing + 1-2 swing; a spec adds 1-2; multi-cam only.

## 7. Direct address and device comedy (Fleabag)
To-camera is psychological defence, not style, so it can become plot. 1 Feasibility-test the device before scripting. 2 Jokes to camera, truth to people; the one monologue not to camera is the emotional peak. 3 The device is the season arc: S1 accomplice, then the camera corners her; S2 one character sees it and escalates until she pushes it away, then asks it not to follow; device done, show done. 4 Trauma as a 2-4 s image, never narrated. Beat: pain-laugh-pain; end on anti-climax.

## 8. Dramedy and jokes carrying tragedy
Douglas: dramedy = continuous characters, backstory, arcs, wry/ironic laughs at no fixed interval. Nothing over three minutes survives joke-to-joke writing; no drama without a comic viewpoint, least of all the tragic episode; slot and format set expectations, so declare them.
Armstrong: the blackest confession is caught by a joke, acceptance as family cruelty; a comic B-line is a thermostat that must also carry plot or it is padding. Prebble: reality beats myth in the room; the funniest pitch occasionally wins.

## 9. Pilot and series format
Default typical-episode pilot (minimal exposition); a premise pilot can't show a normal week. Funny on page one; character appeal first; option before adapting. Series format (10-15 pp, eleven sections): prove legs (100+ episodes), one phrase per character, itself a sample. Pitch 10-15 springboards for an existing show; one big idea + 1-2 spares for a new one; pitch funny.

## 10. The comedy room
Group break > solo draft > group punch-up (roundtable); five rewrites normal. Alts (Landau 2e): drafts swell to 50-60 pp, cut to 30; bank cut jokes (candy bag), burn structural ideas.

| Room model | Method | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Parks & Rec (Schur) | group break, solo episodes; whole-room drafting has diminishing returns | ensemble |
| Mindy Kaling | story-area docs to the platform; 1-2 writers per draft, then room property | many voices |
| Insecure (Rae) | room writes and reads aloud every episode in-house | unified voice |
| Schitt's Creek (Levy) | 4-6 together, strict A/B/C; beats written as dialogue before splitting | precise tone |

Comedy by committee works; drama moments don't (Bays).

## 11. Disagreements
| Issue | A | B | Choose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teachable? | Landau: craft yes, funny is talent | ch. 13 showrunners: processes | process teachable, taste calibrated |
| Group vs solo draft | Schur/Kaling: solo | Rae/Levy: room writes all | single voice: solo; many voices: group + arbiter |
| Table read | Smith/Landau/Lloyd: test jokes | Levy: structure only | test jokes only if they live in lines |
| Act cliffhangers | Smith: every act | Levy: never, emotional stakes | ad breaks: act out; no ads: earned moment |
| New writer speaks? | Lapiduss: not much | Frank: pitch jokes | read the seniors' signals |

## 12. Diagnostic checklist
(29 questions, as in the source)
Premise: 1 Premise a joke machine, or forced wordplay? 2 Predicament sustained or a few pages; when resolved? 3 Two characters contributing the same thing? 4 Expands, not changes, the premise; Too Funny to Pass Up or Genre Shuffle? 5 A device squeezing the cast together?
Structure: 6 A 5-9 beats, subplots 3-5, each changing goal or raising tension? 7 Act One too long (1-5 of 30 pp; 3-act single-A non-pilot only)? 8 Key dramatic moment in the block comedy scene? 9 Predicaments escalating? 10 Strong act breaks: joke or new plot point? 11 A different lead per line; hero driving or towed? 12 Scenes start late, finish early, with a button? 13 Over 2 new sets or 5 total (multi-cam)?
Jokes: 14 Red pen: 2-4 real laughs this page and the first three? 15 Setup has development, no hint? 16 Punch word last, then stop? 17 Topped over twice; toppers in dramedy? 18 Running gags return 2-3 times, last flipped; seeds harvested? 19 Punch won't come: rewrite setup or go literal? 20 Reiss's three conditions? 21 Own 1-2 nuances per character; identifiable with names covered? 22 Laugh quantity and type even? 23 Lines that should be a take, stage business or visual?
Format: 24 Film or tape; pages 27-30/45-50/40-50; layout per format? 25 Production-draft items in the spec (scene numbers, cast lists, slugs, camera, dates); too many cues?
Tone: 26 Dramedy laughs wry/ironic or joke-to-joke? 27 Comedy inside the tragic episode; a joke catching the darkest confession? 28 Comic B-line's plot function (none: cut)? 29 Things this line does at once; fewer than two: cut.

## 13. Workflow
0 Read the target show's scripts. 1 "What if" from the hero's emotion. 2 Springboard, 3-5 funny sentences. 3 Beat sheet. 4 Name predicament, mix, style; else no draft. 5 Outline (5-7 pp) sells; at two-thirds ask what the episode is about (Williams). 6 Draft fast; count laughs. 7 Rewrite story > sequences > scenes > moments > dialogue.

## 14. Training
1 Count and classify laughs in a real script, then yours. 2 Rewrite only the setup of your three weakest punches. 7 Film any unusual device for five minutes first. 10 Punch-up drill: 10-20 alts, keep 5-10, bank the rest.
