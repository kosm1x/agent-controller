# How an episode is made: breaking story, the document chain, notes
**Read this when:** joining or running a writers' room, breaking an episode with a group, writing a leave-behind, beat sheet or outline on deadline, speccing or pitching an existing show, taking or giving notes, rewriting for money, actors or locations, or choosing a room vs a single author.
**Source:** distilled 2026-09-12 from jtydhr88/screenwriting-skills `sw-writers-room` (method only); underlying books: Goldberg & Rabkin *Successful Television Writing* (2003), Douglas *Writing the TV Drama Series* (3e 2011), Landau *The TV Showrunner's Roadmap* (2014/2022), Smith *Writing Television Sitcoms*, Blum (1999-2001), Miller *Your Hero Succeeds*; forewords by Chase, Armstrong, Prebble, Sorkin, Fellowes, Waller-Bridge.
**Pairs with:** knowledge/screenwriting/08-series-structure.md (acts, bottle placement); knowledge/screenwriting/09-series-engine-and-bible.md (pitch document, bible); knowledge/screenwriting/01-workflow-and-story-bible.md; knowledge/screenwriting/12-terms.md

Premises: TV's ladder is built on writers' titles (Douglas); an episode is a product made weekly, in fixed days, for a fixed price, at a fixed length (Goldberg); room performance outweighs your idea and your script.

## 1. The showrunner's duties and veto
A writer empowered to protect the scripts through conception, writing, rewriting, performance, capture and editing (Armstrong). Duties: **decide and communicate** (Goldberg); **be the single voice** (Mazzara: rewrite every word, leave the credit); **hand the director a preppable script on prep day one** (Hanson); **hold final cut** (King; Chase: editor's cut, director's cut, then the showrunner may reorder or drop scenes); **keep drama out of committee** - the room punches comedy only (Bays); ~80% management (Goldberg). Only one EP runs the show: find who decides and answer to them. Staff posture: it is their show; adapt without losing your voice (Goldberg, West).

## 2. Breaking story
Story meeting / "cracking": talk the routes, agree, then whiteboard beat, scene, act. Pick ONE method:

| Method | Source | Procedure | Anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 35 beats, scissors and tape | Chase, *Sopranos* | board beats per line; type A/B/C columns; cut, tape into episode order; retype = outline | A~13, B~13, C 5-6, D few; ~3 months to camera |
| Ten chairs, six-week season | Wells, *ER* | staff pounds out season turns and act-outs; one writer drafts a treatment; two rounds of notes; script | 4 senior + 4 staff + researcher + Wells; ~8 wk/episode |
| Four-act grid, colored cards | Douglas | logline; credibility + rooting-interest tests; three tent-poles; work backward; one card color per line | ~28 scenes/hr: A 12-16, B 8-12, C 3-8 |
| Mini-camp, five-episode pipeline | Lindelof, *Lost* | 3 weeks blue-sky; in production 4 h room then set | ~9 people; five episodes in flight; a bible keeper |
| Movements | Katims, *FNL* | tent-poles only; 4-6-episode runs each driven by one answerable question | Hillian: 10 eps as 1 / 2-4 / 5-7 / 8-10 |
| Emotional line first | West, *Dexter* | break the emotional journey before plot; know episode 12 | eight writers, all February |
| Mega-chart + maybe culture | Prebble, *Succession* | ideas under character names; survivors to a characters x episodes grid; stay open | 8-12 writers, 5-6 h/day, months |

**Worst case, strictly** (Douglas): the antagonist's win against the hero's goal, not a generic disaster; every beat an action ("realizes" is not a scene); antagonist as strong as the hero; worst case at three-quarters; boring cards = boring screen.
**Counter-position** (Mazzara; Gilligan similar): no outline (unshootable; executive notes on it never apply) - a 2-3-page story document plus two-word beats; no A/B/C - ask what the episode is about; cost: minor characters stay minor. **Choose:** approvals, budgeting or newcomer = outline; a showrunner may skip it as a by-product of power, not a better method. Ensemble = A/B/C + cards; single lead = one question.
Room rules: race to act-outs and ending, then improve (Abrams); one problem at a time (Williams); pitch a fix, not a complaint (Gurvitz) vs Lear: name the wrong even without a fix; a pitch refused three times stops (Frank); best episodes come from real stories (Bays); price every change (Miller).

## 3. The document chain with page budgets
- **Leave-behind** (Goldberg): one page, three paragraphs (beginning, middle, end), jacket-copy punch. Two versions: network (conflict + hook) and show (conflict + the regular relationship it touches).
- **Beat sheet / outline**: treatment = prose summary; beat sheet = events per act; outline = scenes to be written; use the show's label. 6-7 scenes/act, 4 acts (2003), 6-20 pages, WGA 7 working days; Douglas: one page per act. Every step = protagonist + goal + opposition; every scene names a location and a shootable action (the line producer budgets from it); nothing the camera cannot see; fix story-day continuity here; a producer's room line goes in verbatim (Bull). Earns story credit; may be locked.
- **First draft**: never silently change approved beats; phone on big departures. **14-day method** (Douglas): 28 beats / 2 scenes a day; reread yesterday, write 2-3 pages, stop and leave, polish later, second scene, stop; the draft arrives pre-edited.
- **Second draft, polish, pages**: revising is not rewriting; polish = small dialogue work; dated colored pages pink, blue, green, changes starred; keep all drafts.
- **Six-week episode** (Douglas, one-hour): outline 1 wk, revise days, draft 2 wk, notes 1-2 d, second draft 1 wk, +1-2 wk polish; Wells's eight weeks = assignment to shootable incl. notes rounds. Sitcom freelance job (Smith 2009): 3-5 weeks in all.

## 4. Spec, research, pitching an existing show
- **Spec** (Goldberg): a show that lasts, won't change, isn't serialized, is read - and dare to break all four; write the best version of the show that is; tone match beats prestige. Douglas: don't spec the show you'll pitch (insiders know the landmines).
- **Research**: read the main title shot by shot (does your story keep its promise?); ask what rises from regulars in stasis, never the running arc; catch each voice; bring an angle only you own.
- **Pitch**: an audition, two minutes. Count per Smith (10-15 springboards), length per Blum (3-5 sentences), delivery per Goldberg (teaser, arcs, act breaks). Not married to it, never angry, stop when they bite, don't buy it back. Think conflict, not case/monster/illness: involvement, stakes, unlike last week, character turn. "Write an outline" = sale; then you are a stenographer.

## 5. Taking and giving notes
**Seven disciplines** (Goldberg): 1 listen - no arguing, explaining, defending; "got it". 2 Ask what problem a note fixes when it creates more than it solves (people give solutions, not problems). 3 Overall notes (tone, character, structure) vs line notes; a "killer" (dislike, no fix) must not leave the room without a proposed fix. 4 Do every line note. 5 Write them down. 6 A hated note: do it well - it gets done with or without you. 7 Ego outside.
Ordering 6 vs 2 (this pack's addition): 6 governs taste notes; 2 governs notes that break something fixed (cast, aired canon, count, budget) - translate solution to problem, offer another solution. Test: does refusing cost someone's taste, or does obeying break structure? In-room behaviour is identical either way. Record, don't type (Douglas).
Metabolize (Rochant via Landau): every word and meaning, digest, return with your own questions. Warm then cold read (Miller): warm readers know the project; cold readers know nothing - ask where the story is going; keep notes that repeat, that you already agreed with, or that make you squirm; fix logic a scene or two upstream. Giving: anonymous reads, two readers each (Souders); a note may come from someone who knows episode +4.

## 6. The staff ladder and step deals (US; dated to source year)
Structure holds; money is stale (WGA minimums renegotiate every three years).

| Rung | Title | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | freelance writer | one episode; no meetings, no office |
| 2 | staff writer | first room; ~1 supervised script; up-or-out; fee offsets salary |
| 3 | story editor / exec story editor | ~2 originals + polishes |
| 4 | producer / co-producer | may rewrite overnight, credit stays |
| 5 | supervising producer | who you pitch to; can assign |
| 6 | creative consultant | prestige, no desk; may be the real showrunner |
| 7 | executive producer / showrunner | section 1 |

Relative money (Douglas 2011): freelance story+teleplay >$30k; step deal story >$12k, draft ~$20k; staff >=$3k/wk; story editor ~$6k/wk + fees. Blum (1998): story 30% / draft 40% / balance.
**Step deal cut-offs** (Douglas): after story and after first draft (voice, meaning, quality); 50% rule - teleplay credit shared if half survives, lost beyond. Sitcoms cut only after the outline. Freelance now = audition scripts, often rewrites: say yes. Never pitch to a story editor; seniors yield credit downward.

## 7. Production limits as creative triggers
Know what the script is about and you know what you can lose (Goldberg). Everything costs; locations change first; 7-12 pages/day.
- **Translate solutions back to problems**: "move scenes to standing sets, cut the chase" hides "pages over on stage, dollars over on stunts" - trade differently, keep the chase. No bunker mentality.
- **Budget bottles** (Sorkin "17 People"; "Pine Barrens"): one night, one building. Told "no money": which two characters owe a confrontation, which season secret moves a step.
- **Actors**: vetoes, dates, bodies; write to the actor; a good actor makes dialogue removable (Fellowes).
- **Alts, freebie take, writing long** (Armstrong); loss list of every cut, repaid later. Unsalvageable: say so, spend on the next.

## 8. Choosing a process
| Process | Example | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Standard US room | *ER*, *Dexter*, *Succession* | 8-12 people, months; ensemble, steady output |
| Single author + substitute room | *Fleabag* | five roles: script editor, story producer, director, actors, editor (the final draft) - scheduled, not hoped for |
| No room, no outline | *Euphoria* | creator-director, autobiographical; exception, not method |
| Season outline + hierarchy | *Gomorrah*, *Le Bureau* | showrunner, seniors, juniors, interns; contractual cadence |
| Comedy break + solo draft + punch-up | *HIMYM* | half-hour; see sitcom doc |
Match process to voice; comedy by committee, drama not (Bays).

## 10. Diagnostics
(35 questions, as in the source: 6 + 6 + 6 + 5 + 9 + 3)
**Before the room** - 1 Episodes watched, title read, franchise in 4 lines, story portable elsewhere (then wrong)? 2 Springboard count, 3-5 sentences each, from stasis not the arc? 3 Pre-pitched; can the listener hire? 4 Ready for subject/killer changes; which one breaks everything? 5 Which kind of room; who runs it? 6 Sets, locations, spellings, headings listed?
**Breaking** - 7 Beats per line (A 12-16, B 8-12, C 3-8, ~35)? 8 Three tent-poles; worst case = antagonist's win? 9 Every beat an action; cards boring? 10 One problem at a time? 11 What does each change buy? 12 Opener "trashy version" not self-deprecation; pitch refused three times?
**Before delivery** - 13 Leave-behind one page/three paragraphs, both versions? 14 6-7 scenes/act, 6-20 pages, location + action per scene? 15 Anything unseeable? 16 Story day, day/night consistent? 17 Showrunner's lines verbatim, no bad temp dialogue? 18 Done early, cooled, delivered on time?
**After notes** - 19 No arguing; "got it"; recorded? 20 Overall vs line; killer answered in the room? 21 Digested; own questions back? 22 Repeats, known weak spots, squirm notes? 23 Cold reader knows where it goes; all line notes done?
**Rewrite and production** - 23a Every diagnostic answered with a location in the revision? 23b Checked against aired/locked episodes (hooks paid, last image picked up, nothing spent early)? 24 What is it about; what can go? 25 Locations essential or decorative; fold-ins? 26 Solutions translated to problems? 27 Bottle: which confrontation, which secret? 28 Alts 0-15, colleagues asked, freebie take? 29 Loss list per cut? 30 Unsalvageable - said so?
**Career** - 31 Office quietest and farthest; hid at home? 32 Writing better or worse than on arrival; discussed? 33 Second income, other job, shared costs?

## 12. Exercises
1 Three main titles shot by shot. 2 Reverse-engineer a beat sheet. 3 Budget note: three exteriors cut, four scenes to standing sets. 4 Second lead has flu: write them out. 5 Notes rehearsal; adopt only the useful. 6 Two-minute pitch. 7 Trashy version, dial back three notches. 8 Stasis list: five impulses, no arc. 9 Pull the B line, fix, reinsert. 10 Ten chairs: 3-5 people, six hours, board to A/B/C to treatment to notes.
